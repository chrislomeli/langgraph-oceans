# Athletic Strength & Conditioning: Rehab-Safe Blueprint

> **Note:** This program was developed in consultation with a foot doctor and is tailored for someone recovering from Achilles tendinopathy with secondary plantar fascia involvement. Always follow your physician's guidance and stop any exercise that causes pain beyond mild discomfort.

---

## Core Protection Rules

These rules apply at all times — during exercise, rehab, and daily life.

**The 90-Degree Floor Baseline.** Never allow your heels to drop below the level of your toes. This prevents end-range dorsiflexion, which compresses the Achilles tendon and over-stretches the plantar fascia. This rule is the foundation of everything else in this program.

**No Barefoot Training.** Perform all exercises in rigid, high-stability court shoes or stiff sneakers. Barefoot or soft-soled footwear forces the foot to over-stabilize and will aggravate both conditions.

**No Heel Striking.** Be deliberate about how your foot contacts the ground during all movement, including walking. Heel striking places sudden load through the plantar fascia and Achilles insertion. Land mid-foot where possible.

**Morning Protocol.** The plantar fascia is typically at its most irritable first thing in the morning. Before placing your foot on the floor after waking, perform 10–15 slow toe extensions while still in bed (gently pulling your toes back toward your shin). This warms the fascia before your first step of the day.

**No Stairmaster.** Avoid the Stairmaster entirely during recovery. It places the Achilles through repeated loaded dorsiflexion — exactly the stress pattern you are protecting against. Return to it only once you are fully pain-free walking.

**Why this split matters.** Performing the heel raise protocol when your stabilizing muscles are fatigued from strength training increases ankle wobble, which shifts load to the outer shin (tibialis anterior) and undermines rehab quality. Separating the sessions protects both.

## Strength Sessions (3 Days/Week)

### Push

**Floor Push-Ups — 3 sets to comfortable failure**
Standard push-up position on the floor. Keep your body in a straight line and lower your chest to just above the floor. Your feet remain relaxed and passive — do not push off your toes. Rest 90 seconds between sets.

**Seated Arnold Press — 3 sets × 10 reps**
Sit fully back against the vertical bench pad to isolate the upper body. Press the dumbbells overhead in the classic Arnold rotation. Your lower legs play no role here — let them rest flat on the floor.

### Pull

**Chest-Supported Dumbbell Row — 3 sets × 8–10 reps**
Set the bench to a 30–45 degree incline and lie face down. Keep your feet resting flat and motionless on the floor for passive support only. Pull the weights toward your hips using your back and biceps, not momentum.

**Single-Arm Dumbbell Row — 3 sets × 10 reps per side**
Place one knee and one hand on the bench for support. Drive your elbow back and up, pulling the dumbbell toward your hip. This complements the chest-supported row by adding a fuller range of motion and independent loading for each side.

### Arms

**Dumbbell Bicep Curls — 3 sets × 10–12 reps**
Stand or sit. Keep your elbows pinned to your sides and curl with a controlled tempo — 2 seconds up, 2 seconds down. Avoid swinging.

**Dumbbell Skull Crushers — 3 sets × 10–12 reps**
Lie flat on the bench. Hold the dumbbells over your chest with arms extended. Bend only at the elbows to lower the weights toward your temples, then press back up. Keep your upper arms vertical throughout. This is one of the easiest tricep movements to dial in with adjustable dumbbells.

**Hanging** 30 seconds X 3




### Core

**Floor Dead Bugs — 3 sets × 10 reps per side**
Lie flat on your back with arms extended toward the ceiling and knees bent at 90 degrees. Slowly extend your opposite arm and leg toward the floor without letting your lower back arch. Your feet never contact the floor during the movement, so zero load passes through the Achilles. Keep your ankles relaxed throughout.

**Bench Leg Lowers — 3 sets × max controlled reps**
Lie on your back on the bench and grip the frame above your head. Lift your legs to 90 degrees, then slowly lower them until they are parallel to the bench surface. Keep your lower back pressed firmly into the pad and your ankles loose.

## Daily Eccentric Heel Raise Protocol

This is the most important element of your Achilles recovery. Eccentric loading — specifically the slow, controlled lowering phase — is the gold-standard evidence-based treatment for Achilles tendinopathy. Perform this every day.

### Setup

**Corner or Wall Support.** Stand inside a tight corner or narrow hallway with your hands resting lightly on both walls. This eliminates side-to-side ankle drift, which is the primary cause of outer-shin burning (tibialis anterior overload).

### The Protocol — 3 sets × 10–15 reps

1. **Rise up** using both legs together. Drive evenly through both feet to reach your highest point — never going past the 90-degree baseline (heels level with toes).
2. **Shift weight** to your injured leg at the top.
3. **Lower slowly** over 3–4 full seconds on the injured leg only.
4. **Transfer back** to both feet and repeat.
5. **Stop the set** 2 reps before your shin begins to burn.

**Hand Deloading.** Actively press down onto the wall or counter with your palms during the descent phase to take approximately 10–20% of your body weight off the ankle. Use more assistance on harder days, less as you improve.

**The Kickstand Regression.** If floating single-leg lowering still causes wobbling, place the toes of your healthy foot about 6 inches behind you. Keep 80% of your weight on the injured foot and use the back foot only for side-to-side balance correction.

### Progression

Once 3 × 15 feels controlled and pain-free for several consecutive days, you can progress by reducing wall support slightly or slowing the descent to 5 seconds. Never progress by adding weight until you are fully pain-free.

## Post-Exercise Relief

### Shin Relief Stretch *(after heel raises)*
Sit on the bench and cross your injured leg over your healthy knee. Cup your hand over the top of your toes and gently pull your foot down and away from your knee — pointing your toes. Hold for 20–30 seconds. This releases the tibialis anterior muscle pump that builds up during the protocol.

### Plantar Fascia Stretch *(morning and after any activity)*
Sit on the edge of your bed or bench. Cross the affected foot over the opposite knee. Grip your toes and pull them gently back toward your shin until you feel a stretch along the arch of your foot. Hold 20–30 seconds. Repeat 2–3 times.

## Pain Guidelines

| Situation | Action |
|-----------|--------|
| Mild discomfort during exercise (1–3/10) | Acceptable — continue |
| Moderate pain during exercise (4–5/10) | Stop the set, rest, reassess |
| Pain above 5/10 | Stop the session entirely |
| Increased soreness the morning after | Reduce reps/sets next session |
| Pain at rest or while walking increases | Consult your foot doctor |
